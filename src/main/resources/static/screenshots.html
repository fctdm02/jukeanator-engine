Home Screen:

Widescreen Landscape (1920x1080)
Portrait (1080x1920)
 
The Home screen is where most people start. The whole music library is laid out as a grid of album covers, sorted by either artist or album title, with a letter bar that lets you jump straight to any part of the alphabet instead of paging through everything. Tap an album cover to open it and see its full track listing, then tap a song to add it to the queue.

 

Across the top of every screen you will always see the song that is currently playing, your available credits and the location's logo. The large, touch-friendly tabs along the bottom (Home, Search, Hot Here, Genres and Queue) mean any part of the jukebox is only one tap away.
 
The Legacy view of the Home screen will feel familiar to anyone who has used a classic CD jukebox. Each panel shows an album's cover art alongside its complete, scrollable track listing, so a song can be picked with a single tap right from the grid. Bars next to each song show how popular it is, which takes the guesswork out of choosing a song from an album you don't know well.

 

A toggle on the Home screen switches between the album-cover view and the Legacy view at any time. The operator chooses which view the jukebox starts in, and the jukebox returns to it after a period of inactivity.

Search Screen:

Widescreen Landscape (1920x1080)
Portrait (1080x1920)
 
The Search screen finds artists, albums and songs from whatever you type, using a large on-screen keyboard designed for touch screens. Results appear side-by-side in separate Artists, Albums and Songs columns, and each column pages independently, so you can quickly narrow in on exactly what you are looking for. Tap an artist to see all of their albums, tap an album to see its tracks, or tap a song to play it.

 

Because everything is done on-screen, JukeANator never needs a physical keyboard or mouse, which is exactly what you want in a bar, pool hall or restaurant where the jukebox is housed in a cabinet.
 
The Hot Here screen shows what is popular at this location: the artists, albums and songs that your own customers play the most. Like Search, the results are presented in Artists, Albums and Songs columns, and can be sorted by popularity or by name.

 

This is the quickest way for a patron to find a crowd favorite, and a great way to discover what everyone else in the room has been listening to.

Genre Details Screen:

Widescreen Landscape (1920x1080)
Portrait (1080x1920)
 
The Genres screen comes in handy when you are in the mood for a particular style of music. It presents every genre in the library as a large, easy-to-tap tile, regardless of how many genres or albums are in the jukebox.

 

Tapping a genre opens the Genre Details screen, which shows the artists, albums and songs in that genre in separate columns, sortable by popularity or by name. From there you can drill into any artist or album, or play a song directly. Genres are determined by how the music is organized on disk or by the songs' own tags, so the operator stays in control of how the library is grouped.
 
The Queue screen shows the songs that are going to be played, in order, along with each song's priority, album art and artist. A "Show History" switch changes the list to the songs that have already played, with the time each one started, so you can find that great song you heard a few minutes ago.

 

Patrons can also spend credits to move a song up or down in the queue, or to remove a song, with the cost of each action clearly shown on its button before anything is spent. When adding a song, a "Priority Play" option lets a patron jump ahead of other selections for a few extra credits.

Admin Screen:

Widescreen Landscape (1920x1080)
Portrait (1080x1920)
 
The Admin screen is the operator's control center and is protected by an admin login. It shows the full song queue with buttons to skip to the next song, pause, play a selected song, move songs up or down, remove songs, flush or shuffle the queue, and load or save playlists. The queue can also be locked, and an entire album can be queued at once.

 

For the business side, the Admin screen provides a Financial Ledger of cash, card and mobile revenue (including the owner and operator split), a View Activity log of what has happened on the jukebox, and buttons to add or remove credits by hand.

 

Lastly, there are tools for managing the jukebox itself. You can rescan the music library for new songs, edit an album's details, reset play statistics, add admin users, update the location's settings and logo, and view the license.
